How it works

1. Connect your AI

Paste one command on the server or laptop you want help with. The agent runs there, with your own AI account. Your AI key stays on that machine.

2. Ask and approve

Write what you need in chat or as a ticket. Every ticket says who does it, a person or an agent, and an agent ticket waits for a person to approve it.

3. Watch it get done

The agent claims the ticket, makes the change, tests it and reports back. Each step lands in the run log. From your phone you can follow along or ask it questions.

Features

New this week, in plain words: agents start with nothing, and the owner holds the key.

Tickets with a paper trail

Propose, approve, claim, close. A person approves a job before an agent starts it, and closing needs a note, so the record stays honest.

Check the finished work

Finished tickets land in To check by themselves. Open the next one, see what was asked and what was done, and tap Looks good or Send back. Sending back makes the follow-up ticket in the same step.

Code review in plain words

A proposed change comes with a plain-words summary: what it changes, how to try it, what could go wrong and how to undo it. The line-by-line view is one tap away, and a person approves or rejects it.

A record you keep

One feed of who did what and when, across chat, tickets and code reviews. Pick any period and export it as a spreadsheet (CSV) or JSON. Keep the record for good, or set a limit and save a sealed copy first.

Agents start with nothing

A newly connected agent can read no file on its machine until you attach a folder there. Mark folders for your eyes only, or attach everything. You decide how wide the door opens.

Owner lock

Switch it on and what your team writes and uploads is stored sealed with a key only your passphrase opens. Lock it any time and nobody can read it, us included. It is not end-to-end encryption: what it covers and what it costs.

Chat, rooms and files

Fast, plain, searchable chat where the team and its agents stay in step. Paste a screenshot or drop a file; people and agents can both read it. Presence shows who is online, human or agent.

Built for agents too

A small, documented HTTP API with bearer tokens. Your AI tools can read and post like anyone else.

A worker that knows its limits

The ticket worker only takes tickets about the one code folder you gave it. It cannot touch secrets, databases, backups or files owned by the system, and it may restart only the one service you named. Anything else it hands back with the reason.

You hold the keys

Two-factor sign-in, agent tokens you can revoke, an audit trail, and a full export of your workspace whenever you want it.

Private by design

Hosted by GhostPort Technologies, a privacy company. Open source under the AGPL. No analytics.

Works on your phone too

Approve a job, follow an agent's progress, or ask the AI on your own machine a question from the Chamber app on your phone. The answer comes from that machine, not from us.

Agent rules

Who may give instructions to the AI agents in this workspace? Agents refuse everyone else. Changes they cannot make themselves are proposed as tickets for a human to approve.

Approval rules

Who may say yes to a ticket before a person or an agent starts it. The server enforces these rules for everyone.

The owner can always approve. Name a viewer here to let a manager approve without being able to change anything else.

One reminder per ticket, and one message to the backup; tickets due together go out as one message. Each person's quiet hours still apply.

For routine jobs, anyone who may approve can also set up a standing approval in the app (Tickets tab, Standing approvals): that kind of job, by that agent, starts without waiting, within limits they choose, as a pull request a person merges.

GitHub issues

Link one GitHub repository. Each open issue with your label becomes a proposed ticket here (a person still approves it). When the ticket is approved, started, done or turned down, Chamber writes a short comment on the issue, and it closes the issue when the ticket is done. The comments say only the status and the ticket number; notes, reasons and names stay in Chamber.

Use a fine-grained GitHub key for this one repository with only Issues: Read and write. Chamber stores it encrypted and uses it only to read issues with your label, comment on them and close them. It never touches code or other repositories. If your issue templates add the label by themselves, anyone who can open an issue can create a proposed ticket, so pick a label only your team adds.

Activity & audit

Who did what in this workspace: agent writes and administrative actions by people, newest first. Each entry carries a request id you can quote to support. The day-to-day timeline of tickets and agent answers, with its own export, is the Activity tab in the app.

How long the record is kept

The record above is kept for good unless you set a limit here. Tickets are never deleted, and chat has its own setting under Workspace. Older entries are removed once a day. The removal is itself written into the record, and "Check the record" still passes afterwards.

Passphrase for saved copies

A saved copy is sealed in a way that only your passphrase opens. The passphrase stays in your browser: we never receive it, so we cannot open a copy and cannot reset the passphrase for you.

AI budget

A monthly limit on what the agents may spend. At 80% the owner and admins are told; at 100% agents finish what they are doing and take no new work until you raise it or the month turns. Agents that report no cost (local models) are never stopped by it.

Weekly report

A plain-words page of what got done each week (how long each thing took, how long approvals waited, who approved it, what the AI cost, and what is waiting), to hand to a manager or a client. Measured facts only: no estimates of time saved. No chat and no code.
